What to know before you ship it

    Where the rules came from, and where the two sources disagree

    Everything this page enforces was read out of a source on 2026-09-01, not recalled.

    vixie/cron, crontab.5: minute 0-59, hour 0-23, day of month 1-31 (or $ for the last day), month 0-12 or names, day of week 0-7 where both 0 and 7 are Sunday. Ranges are inclusive, lists are comma separated, and a step follows a range as /n.

    vixie/cron, entry.c and cron.c: the man page says the OR rule applies when both day fields are "not *", but the flag is set on the field's first character, so */2 in the day of month field counts as a star. The match is (DOM_STAR|DOW_STAR) ? (dom && dow) : (dom || dow). Implement the sentence and you get a different program from the one it describes.

    GitHub Actions docs: five fields, minute 0-59, hour 0-23, day of month 1-31, month 1-12 or JAN-DEC, day of week 0-6 or SUN-SAT. "By default, scheduled workflows run in UTC." "The shortest interval you can run scheduled workflows is once every 5 minutes." @daily and friends are not supported. Scheduled workflows run only on the default branch. The schedule event "can be delayed during periods of high loads", and "High load times include the start of every hour."

    GitHub Actions docs, on the deleted hour: "during DST spring-forward transitions, scheduled workflows in skipped hours advance to the next valid time. For example, a 2:30 AM schedule advances to 3:00 AM." A cron daemon walking wall clock times does not do this; it simply misses the fire. The toggle above switches between the two.

    So the portable expression is the intersection: day of week 0-6, month 1-12, no $, no @daily, and never both day fields restricted unless you actually meant OR.
