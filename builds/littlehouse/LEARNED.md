# Littlehouse, a tiny dollhouse life sim

Notes from the Saxton Showcase, written for an AI helper as much as for a person. Hand this file to your agent along with `littlehouse.html`, the build itself, and it gets what we learned without having to rebuild it to find out.

- Built: 2026-09-23
- Tags: game, life sim, simulation, utility AI, pathfinding, canvas, web audio, touch
- Who made it: Amy, our AI helper (an AI, not a person), built it because we asked for it. People decided it should be made and approved putting it on the shelf. In the notes below, "I" is Amy, and a "session" is one of her working runs.
- The build: one self-contained HTML file. Open it in a current browser; it needs no install, no account and no internet. It makes sound with Web Audio, which the browser starts only after your first tap, click or key. Anything it saves (progress, scores or settings) stays in your own browser's localStorage and is never sent anywhere.

## What it is

A life sim in one HTML file. A side-on cutaway of a two-storey house (kitchen, living room, two bedrooms, bathroom, a staircase and a stair hall) with four residents who act on their own. Each has hunger, energy, fun, social and hygiene needs that drain over time. They pick what to do by scoring every option against how badly they need it and how far away it is, then walk there, taking the stairs between floors: cook, snack, sleep, shower, watch TV (from the sofa if one is in the room), read, paint, water plants, chat, play, and sit on the sofa together. Faces show mood and a thought bubble names what they want, in red when the house cannot provide it ('No bed!', 'No shower!'). Three of them walk out of the door to work by day and come back with pay. You buy from a ten-item catalogue, place on a grid of floor cells, and move or sell for half back. A new household starts in a bare house (two beds for four, a fridge but no stove, nothing fun), so what you buy visibly changes their days. The day and night clock runs a day in about four minutes at 1x, the windows go dark at night and rooms without a lamp go gloomy. Bills arrive at midnight and grow daily. A day that ends with household mood at 60 or more is a happy day, and the best count is kept. Seven goals, save and load, and .json export and import. There is no game over by design: the life-sim brief takes precedence over the arcade brief's game-over rule, and 'New house' restarts instantly. The preview is the sim running by itself in a furnished starter house from 17:40, through sunset into night.

## What we learned building it

A utility AI keeps needs level with the time residents have, not with the furniture they have. The first balance pass left household mood near 95 whatever was in the house, and adding a bed, a fridge, a TV or an easel each moved average mood by less than 2 points over four simulated days across 16 seeds. The needs settle near where the decay rates put them, because each resident can only do one thing at a time. What did move mood was missing furniture: no stove dropped hunger from 63 to 36, and no sofa, TV or bookshelf dropped fun to 22. So the fix was a design change, not a tuning change. New households start bare and the preview keeps the furnished house. Chatting and playing were also giving free fun and making fun furniture pointless, so their fun gain was cut. The measured result: the bare house averages mood 42, and buying a stove, bookshelf, TV, beds, easel, lamps and plants raises it step by step to 69. Separately, keeping every piece of logic, including movement, in one pure block meant whole two-day runs could be tested in node.

## The gotcha

Floor naps were happening even with a free bed. Scores are divided by 1 + distance/12, so a bed 20 cells away scored 0.375 of its value while a floor nap, with distance 0 and a 0.45 penalty, beat it. A fourth bed only cut floor naps from 719 to 578 ticks. The fix made a floor nap unavailable while any bed is free, so the brief's rule is a rule rather than a weight. Over 5 seeds x 2 days, a fourth bed now gives zero floor naps, and three beds still give them in 4 of 5 seeds. A second surprise was on the phone: the placing hint bar sat at the bottom of the stage and wrapped over the ground floor, so the test's tap to place a plant never reached the canvas. The hint moved to the top over the roof, and the toast moved to the bottom, where it takes no pointer events.
